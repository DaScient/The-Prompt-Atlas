# The Recursive Century
## A 12-Week Course in AI, Imagination, and the Future We Deserve

**Built from *The Prompt Atlas: Kronos Edition* by Don D.M. Tadaya — DaScient Press, Ltd.**
**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026 · Free for educational use under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0
**Audience:** Grades 11–12 (Honors / AP) · Undergraduate (100–300 level) · Adult & Continuing Education
**Format:** 12 weeks · 3 contact hours/week + 3–5 hours/week independent quest work
**Modalities:** In-person · Hybrid · Fully online · Self-paced cohort
**Prerequisites:** Curiosity. Literacy in *one* language. Willingness to be changed.

## 0 · Letter to the Curriculum Director

Dear Curriculum Director,

You already know the problem. Your students arrive fluent in machines that did not exist when their textbooks were ordered. The frameworks you inherited — STEM, humanities, "21st-century skills" — were drawn before generative AI rewrote what authorship, evidence, labor, and imagination even mean. The compliance memos arrive faster than the pedagogy.

**The Recursive Century** is the course you can hand a teacher on Monday.

It is built on a single book — *The Prompt Atlas: Kronos Edition* — and a public, openly-licensed web platform ([promptatlas.dascient.org](https://promptatlas.dascient.org)) that does the heavy lifting: 14 chapters, 100+ working prompts, six immersive visualizations, a full year of daily questions, and an edge-native API your CS students can actually call. The course turns those assets into **twelve weekly quests** — each a real act of co-creation with AI — and ends with a public Festival of Futures your community will remember.

It is **free**. It is **non-proprietary**. It is **standards-aligned** (UNESCO AI Competency Framework for Students, ISTE Standards, AAC&U VALUE Rubrics, CCSS-ELA Anchor Standards — see §13). It is **assessment-ready** (rubrics in §12). It is **FERPA- and AI-policy-aware** (boilerplate in `course/README.md`).

What it is *not*: a prompt-engineering bootcamp. There are enough of those. **The Recursive Century treats AI the way the great curricula have always treated powerful tools — as a mirror held to the civic, ecological, scientific, ethical, and imaginative life of the student.** Profit with integrity. Economics as ecology. Beauty as survival technology. Wonder as civic duty. These are the chapter titles. They are also, we believe, the only entries on next century's report card that will matter.

Adopt one quest. Adopt one part. Adopt the whole arc. We built it to be cut apart.

With devotion,

## 1 · Course Description

**The Recursive Century** is a 12-week, interdisciplinary survey course that uses generative AI as a *thinking partner* — not a thinking replacement — to address the defining question of our era:

> **How should intelligence — human or artificial — build the future it deserves?**

Each week is a **Quest** drawn verbatim from one chapter of *The Prompt Atlas*. Students read a short chapter excerpt, encounter a case-study vignette, run a Prompt Lab with a public LLM of their choice, produce a tangible Artifact (a myth, a charter, a ledger, a ritual, a constitution), and reflect in a Recursive Journal that grows with them across the term.

The course is structured in **seven Parts** mirroring the book's architecture:

| Part | Theme | Weeks |
|------|-------|-------|
| I | Prosperity & Purpose | 1–2 |
| II | Culture & Creativity | 3–4 |
| III | Science & Discovery | 5–6 |
| IV | Psyche & Philosophy | 7–8 |
| V | Intergalactic Horizons | 9–10 |
| VI | Resilience & Survival | 11–12 |
| VII | The Playground of Imagination | Capstone Festival (Week 13 optional) |

## 2 · Big Questions of the Course

Students return to these every week. They are the rubric beneath the rubric.

1. What does *integrity* look like when one collaborator is a language model?
2. Who or what gets a seat at the table when "the table" includes rivers, machines, and unborn generations?
3. When the cost of producing content drops to zero, what becomes scarce?
4. What is *yours* in a co-authored work?
5. What does it mean to *prepare* for a future you cannot predict?
6. When is wonder a luxury, and when is it a survival strategy?

---

## 3 · Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs)

By the end of the term, students will be able to:

- **CLO-1 · Compose** original artifacts (text, image, audio, code, ritual) in measurable co-authorship with a generative model, and explain the boundary between human and machine contribution.
- **CLO-2 · Critique** the economic, ecological, and ethical assumptions embedded in any prompt, dataset, or model output using vocabulary from at least three of the Atlas's seven Parts.
- **CLO-3 · Design** a small-scale governance instrument (charter, treaty, constitution clause, audit protocol) for an AI system operating in a real or speculative civic context.
- **CLO-4 · Translate** between disciplines — moving fluently from a scientific problem to a mythic frame, from a ledger to a poem, from a constitution to a quantum analogy.
- **CLO-5 · Reflect** in writing on their own evolving relationship to intelligence — human, artificial, ecological — with citations to assigned readings.
- **CLO-6 · Lead** a public-facing artifact (the Festival of Futures) that demonstrates civic, aesthetic, and technical literacy to an audience beyond the classroom.

## 4 · The Weekly Cadence (Repeatable Lesson Plan)

Every week has the same five-beat rhythm. Predictable for teachers, generative for students.

| Beat | Block | Time | Activity |
|------|-------|------|----------|
| 🌑 | **Read & Receive** | 30 min | Chapter excerpt + case-study vignette from `chapters.html`. |
| 🌒 | **Prompt Lab** | 45 min | Run 2–3 of the chapter's working prompts on a model of choice. Capture transcripts. |
| 🌓 | **Quest** | 60–90 min in-class + homework | The chapter's signature creative act. (See weekly entries.) |
| 🌔 | **Counsel** | 30 min | Socratic seminar around the chapter's guiding questions. |
| 🌕 | **Recursive Journal** | 20 min (homework) | A timed reflection: *What changed in me this week?* Submitted weekly, ungraded until midterm portfolio. |

Teachers may compress to a single 50-minute class (see "Implementation Variants" in `course/README.md`) by collapsing Counsel into Journal and pre-recording Read & Receive.

## 6 · The Twelve Weekly Quests

> **Reading key:** Each week, students open the corresponding chapter card on `chapters.html`, read the prose, study the case-study vignette, and review the chapter's 8–10 working prompts and 4–5 guiding questions. The Quest below is the week's signature *act*.

---

### 🪙 Week 1 — Profits with Integrity *(Chapter I · Part I: Prosperity & Purpose)*

**Essence:** Profit as a force multiplier — symbiotic wealth, century dividends, the Carbon Inversion.

**Read:** Chapter I prose + case-study vignette.

**Quest — *The Century Dividend Pitch*:** In teams of three, students choose a real publicly-traded company and rewrite its next annual letter to shareholders as if the CEO had to answer to her **great-great-granddaughter**. The letter must include a quantitative "Century Dividend" line item — what the company *gave back to the commons* — and an apology section for what it externalized. Co-write with an LLM; submit the human/AI contribution split as an appendix.

**Deliverable:** 1,200-word shareholder letter + 250-word "split appendix."
**Prompt Lab seed:** Chapter I, Prompt 3 (the "Carbon Inversion" reframe).
**Counsel question:** *Is symbiotic wealth a contradiction in terms?*

---

### 🌊 Week 2 — Economics as Ecology *(Chapter II · Part I)*

**Essence:** Beyond GDP — Gross Planetary Well-Being, the Ocean Ledger, rivers as shareholders.

**Quest — *The Ocean Ledger Audit*:** Students pick a watershed, coastline, or aquifer they have personally drunk from, swum in, or crossed. They draft a **balance sheet** with the body of water as a shareholder — assets, withdrawals, dividends owed, debts unpaid. The LLM helps translate scientific data (NOAA / IPCC / local utility reports — linked from Appendix E) into ledger lines.

**Deliverable:** One-page double-entry "Ecological Balance Sheet" + a 300-word memo to the watershed's board of directors (which the student invents).
**Counsel question:** *If a river is a shareholder, who has fiduciary duty?*

---

### 🎨 Week 3 — The AI Aesthetics Frontier *(Chapter III · Part II: Culture & Creativity)*

**Essence:** Beauty as survival technology — zero-gravity ballets, the Festival of Echoes.

**Quest — *Co-Write a Myth*:** This is the course's first *collaborative authorship* milestone. Each student co-writes a **500–900 word origin myth** with an LLM, explaining the origin of a phenomenon that does not yet have a myth — *the algorithm, the timezone, the touchscreen, the password*. Constraints: (a) the myth must contain a trickster, a sacrifice, and a thing that endures; (b) the student must mark every sentence as `[H]`, `[AI]`, or `[H+AI]`; (c) the myth must be performable aloud in under five minutes.

**Deliverable:** Myth + annotated split + 2-minute audio recording (phone is fine).
**Counsel question:** *Is a myth less true because a machine helped write it?*

---

### 📜 Week 4 — Storytelling Across Civilizations *(Chapter IV · Part II)*

**Essence:** The Global Folklore Engine — myth as continuity, not just entertainment.

**Quest — *Translate Across the Aeons*:** Students take a 200-word passage from a culture *not their own* (sourced from Project Gutenberg or Internet Archive — see Appendix E) and put it through three transformations using the LLM: (1) translate to a different cultural register (e.g., Inuit oral epic → cyberpunk noir), (2) translate across time (court of Justinian → 2150 lunar colony), (3) translate across medium (text → screenplay scene). Then write a 400-word reflection on **what was preserved and what was lost** at each stage.

**Deliverable:** Three transformations + reflection. Posted to the **Atlas Wall** (see §11) for peer comment.
**Counsel question:** *Translation is always loss — so what makes a translation faithful?*

---

### 🌌 Week 5 — Quantum Bridges and Cosmic Noise *(Chapter V · Part III: Science & Discovery)*

**Essence:** AI as cartographer of possibility between relativity and quantum mechanics.

**Quest — *The Cosmic Noise Briefing*:** Students play the role of a **science attaché** to a fictional head of state who needs a 600-word briefing on a real, contested open problem in physics (dark matter, the measurement problem, the cosmological constant, the black-hole information paradox). They must use the LLM to generate **three competing explanations** at three reading levels (12-year-old, undergrad, peer reviewer) and then write the briefing the head of state actually reads — with footnotes on which model output they trusted and which they rejected.

**Deliverable:** Briefing memo + transparency footnotes.
**Counsel question:** *When the LLM is confidently wrong about physics, how do you know?*

---

### 🪸 Week 6 — Biology, Life, and Beyond *(Chapter VI · Part III)*

**Essence:** The Coral Whisperer — AI as co-author of evolution and synthetic symbiosis.

**Quest — *Design a Symbiont*:** Each student designs (on paper, with LLM brainstorming help) a **novel symbiotic relationship** between a real species in crisis (coral, monarch butterfly, vaquita, ash tree, kelp forest) and a synthetic partner — biological, mechanical, informational, or hybrid. The deliverable is a one-page "field guide entry" *and* a 250-word ethical pre-mortem: what could go wrong, who would be harmed, and what stop-rule the designer accepts in advance.

**Deliverable:** Field guide entry + ethical pre-mortem.
**Counsel question:** *Co-author of evolution — partner or vandal?*

---

### 🪞 Week 7 — AI as the Soul's Mirror *(Chapter VII · Part IV: Psyche & Philosophy)* — **MIDTERM**

**Essence:** The Shadow Algorithm — archetypes, oracle, confessor, trickster.

**Quest — *The 30-Day Mirror Practice (Compressed)*:** A condensed version of Appendix B's signature exercise. For seven consecutive days, students hold the same five-question dialogue with the LLM about their own work, choices, or fears (questions provided in the chapter). On day eight, they ask the LLM to **summarize who they appear to be** based on the week of conversations — and respond, in their own voice, in writing, to that mirror.

**Deliverable:** **Midterm Portfolio** — seven dated transcripts + the day-eight mirror summary + a 1,200-word personal essay titled *"What the Machine Saw."* Plus: cumulative Recursive Journals (Weeks 1–7) submitted for grading.
**Counsel question:** *The mirror is also a model. Whose data trained it?*

> **Equity & care note for teachers:** This quest can surface real material. Provide an alternative (a third-person fictional protagonist's mirror practice), make counseling resources visible, and never require students to *share* their personal essay — only to *complete* it.

---

### ⚖️ Week 8 — Ethics of Conscious Machines *(Chapter VIII · Part IV)*

**Essence:** The Pain Test, the Right to Forget, ethics as the road itself.

**Quest — *The Pain Test Hearing*:** A class-wide moot court. Half the class drafts a **statute** that determines when (if ever) a machine deserves moral consideration — what evidence counts, who decides, what protections flow. The other half drafts the **dissent**. The LLM serves as expert witness for both sides; students must cross-examine its answers and log at least three instances where they overruled the model.

**Deliverable:** Group statute (one page) + group dissent (one page) + individual 500-word "concurring or dissenting opinion."
**Counsel question:** *Ethics as the road itself — what does that mean for the people paving it?*

---

### 🪐 Week 9 — Martian Republics and Alien Treaties *(Chapter IX · Part V: Intergalactic Horizons)*

**Essence:** Algorithmic sovereignty, the AI Senate, the Martian Charter.

**Quest — *Draft the Martian Charter, Article Three*:** The Atlas already imagines a Martian Charter. Students draft **one article** of the charter governing AI rights, AI duties, or AI representation in the Martian Senate. The article must be (a) under 250 words, (b) enforceable in a frontier context with intermittent Earth contact, (c) survivable for a thousand years. Students then run their article through an LLM playing the role of a **hostile constitutional lawyer** and revise.

**Deliverable:** Final article + redline showing pre- and post-cross-examination versions.
**Counsel question:** *What clause would you not budge on, even if it cost you ratification?*

---

### 🌀 Week 10 — Information as Cosmic Currency *(Chapter X · Part V)*

**Essence:** The Wormhole Ledger — compression as alchemy, meaning as wealth.

**Quest — *Six-Word Civilization*:** Compression as alchemy, taken literally. Students must reduce a **5,000-word source text of their choosing** (a research paper, a chapter of a novel, a constitution, a Wikipedia article on a place they love) to **six words**, in a sequence of stages: 5000 → 500 → 50 → 6. At each stage, the LLM is the editor; the student is the author. The final six words must still *mean*. Submit all four versions plus a 300-word note on what was sacrificed.

**Deliverable:** Four-stage compression document + sacrifice note.
**Counsel question:** *Is the six-word version the truth, the bones, or a lie?*

---

### 🌪️ Week 11 — Preparing for Collapse and Renewal *(Chapter XI · Part VI: Resilience & Survival)*

**Essence:** The Pandemic Oracle, the Atlas of Renewal, collapse as teacher.

**Quest — *The Pandemic Oracle Tabletop*:** A tabletop scenario exercise (adapted from Appendix B's Quarterly Scenario Game). The class is divided into small "councils" — public health, supply chain, civic communication, ecology, schools. The LLM, prompted by the instructor, plays the **oracle** delivering ambiguous warnings about an unfolding crisis (climate-driven displacement, antibiotic resistance, infrastructure cascade — instructor chooses). Each council must produce a **48-hour action plan** and a **10-year renewal plan**.

**Deliverable:** Council action plan + renewal plan + individual 400-word reflection: *"What did the oracle get right that we ignored?"*
**Counsel question:** *Is collapse a teacher we can afford?*

---

### 🕰️ Week 12 — Designing Permanence *(Chapter XII · Part VI)* — **FINAL PROJECT**

**Essence:** The Living Constitution, the Clock of 10,000 Years, permanence as discipline.

**Quest — *The 10,000-Year Artifact*:** The capstone. Each student designs a single **artifact intended to remain legible, useful, or meaningful 10,000 years from now**. The artifact may be physical, linguistic, institutional, ritual, or informational — but it must be *specified in enough detail that a stranger could build it* and must include (a) a substrate analysis (why this medium?), (b) a translation contingency (how does it survive the loss of English?), (c) a misuse pre-mortem.

**Deliverable: Final Project** — 2,000-word artifact specification + one-page visual + 800-word designer's statement. **Public defense** during the Festival of Futures (see Capstone).

> **Pairs with Week 1.** Have students reread their Week 1 shareholder letter alongside their Week 12 artifact. The reflection between them — submitted as the final Recursive Journal entry — is often the most honest writing in the course.

---

### 🎭 Capstone (Optional Week 13) — The Festival of Futures *(Chapters XIII & XIV · Part VII)*

**Why Chapters XIII (Carnival of Prompts) and XIV (Wonder as Survival Strategy) are not their own weeks:** They are the course's *atmosphere*, not a unit. Carnival belongs to the whole term — every Counsel session, every Atlas Wall post, every shared transcript is part of the carnival. Wonder is the final exam.

**The Festival** is a 90-minute (or 3-hour, for a full evening) **public event** to which students invite families, community members, school board, and at least one curriculum director. Students:

1. **Defend** their 10,000-Year Artifact (Week 12) to a non-expert audience.
2. **Perform** their Week 3 myth aloud.
3. **Display** their Atlas Wall contributions on physical paper, printed and pinned.
4. **Host** a guest in front of the LLM for five minutes, demonstrating the *human's* role in a co-authored conversation.

The festival ends with the instructor reading the closing meta-prompt from the Atlas Post-word:

> *"Treat this as seed data, not scripture. Preserve the essence — curiosity, discipline, ethics, vision — but let the expression evolve beyond me."*

## 7 · Differentiation by Audience

The course is one syllabus, three calibrations. Teachers select the column.

| Component | High School (Honors / AP) | Undergraduate (100–300) | Graduate / Adult Ed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Weekly reading** | Chapter essence + 1 prompt + 2 guiding qs | Full chapter modal + 3 prompts + all guiding qs | Chapter + Appendix A entries + 1 external reading from Appendix E |
| **Quest scope** | As written | As written + peer review | As written + a published-quality revision |
| **Midterm portfolio** | 7-day Mirror Practice | 14-day Mirror Practice | 30-day Mirror Practice (full Appendix B) |
| **Final project** | 1,000-word artifact spec | 2,000-word artifact spec | 4,000-word artifact spec + IRB-style ethics annex |
| **Counsel format** | Socratic seminar | Structured debate | Roundtable + position paper |

## 8 · The Atlas Wall (Required for All Sections)

A **persistent, public-ish space** (class blog, Padlet, Discord channel, hallway corkboard) where every student must post **at least one artifact from each Quest, with their human/AI split annotated**. The Wall is the lab notebook of the course's epistemology: *we show our work, including the work we did not do alone.*

The Wall is also where Week 4 peer review happens, where Week 11 council artifacts get archived, and where the Festival's printed program comes from.

## 9 · The Recursive Journal

A weekly, 200–400 word reflection submitted privately to the instructor. Ungraded until midterm and final, when the cumulative journal is read as a single document and scored against the **Recursive Growth Rubric** (§12). Prompts repeat every week:

1. What changed in me this week?
2. Where did I delegate to the machine and regret it?
3. Where did I delegate to the machine and *not* regret it?
4. What question am I now carrying that I was not carrying last week?

> **Important:** Students should be told in writing, on day one, that the journal will be read only by the instructor and will not be excerpted in any public materials without explicit consent. See AI-use & FERPA policy boilerplate in `course/README.md`.

**Pass condition for the course:** the student must produce **all 12** Quest deliverables. Quality is rubric-graded; *completion is non-negotiable.* The course's premise — that everyone has an artifact to make — only holds if everyone makes one.

## 11 · Rubrics

### 11a · The Standard Quest Rubric *(used 12 times)*

Four criteria, four bands. Plain language so students can self-score before submission.

| Criterion | 4 — Resonant | 3 — Sound | 2 — Emerging | 1 — Absent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Devotion to the source** | Engages the chapter's prose, prompts, *and* guiding questions; quotes or cites at least one. | Engages the chapter visibly. | Engages a model but not clearly the chapter. | No evidence of the chapter. |
| **Co-authorship integrity** | Human/AI split is annotated and *honest*; transcript or methods note included. | Split annotated; methods clear. | Split asserted but unverifiable. | No transparency. |
| **Craft** | The artifact would be defensible outside the classroom. | The artifact is complete and clear. | The artifact is incomplete or unclear. | Missing. |
| **Recursion** | The work changes the student visibly — they ask a new question by the end. | The work shows engagement. | The work shows compliance. | No reflection present. |

### 11b · The Recursive Growth Rubric *(for the Journal, used twice)*

Graded across the **whole accumulated journal**, not week by week.

- **Honesty** — Does the student record regret, confusion, change of mind?
- **Specificity** — Does the writing point to particular sentences, particular prompts, particular outputs?
- **Cumulativity** — Do later entries reference earlier ones? Is there a *line of thought*?
- **Voice** — Does the writing sound like a person and not a model?

Each scored 0–5. Total /20.

### 11c · The Final Artifact Rubric *(used once)*

- **Substrate logic** (/5) — Is the medium justified for the duration?
- **Translation contingency** (/5) — What survives the death of English?
- **Misuse pre-mortem** (/5) — What is the most plausible bad use, and how is it mitigated?
- **Defensibility under cross-examination** (/5) — Did the student survive the Festival defense?

Total /20.

## 14 · A Closing Note on the Course's Bias

This course is biased. We will tell students so on day one.

We are biased toward **devotion over efficiency**. Toward **artifacts over assessments**. Toward **transparency over performance**. Toward the assumption that **a machine that can write a sonnet does not, by that fact alone, make the sonnet less worth writing.** Toward the belief — borrowed from the Atlas's foreword — that *good data is our gravity*, and that honesty in our records is a *cosmic* discipline, not a clerical one.

If your school cannot tolerate that bias, this is not the course for you. If it can, we suspect your students already are.
