## Why This Course, Why Now

You have already had the meeting. The one where AI was discussed and no one in the room had a curriculum to point at — only a policy memo. This is the curriculum.

**The Recursive Century** is built on a single, published book (*The Prompt Atlas: Kronos Edition*) and its free, public companion site. It does not require:

- a vendor contract
- a paid LLM subscription (free tiers suffice for every quest)
- a one-to-one device program (the course works on shared lab time)
- the book itself (the web platform contains every text the syllabus cites)
- any consulting hours from us (though we offer them free for pilot sites — see below)

It does require:

- **3 contact hours per week** for 12 weeks (compressible — see *Implementation Variants*)
- **one teacher** who has read at least Part I of the Atlas and is willing to keep their own Recursive Journal
- a **written AI-use policy** for your institution (we provide boilerplate below)

## Implementation Variants

The syllabus is written for a **3-contact-hour week** with 3–5 hours of independent quest work. Compress as needed:

| Variant | Contact time/week | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| **45-minute single class** | 45 min × 5 days OR 1 × 45 + flipped | Read & Receive becomes a pre-recorded clip; Prompt Lab + Quest collapse to one block; Counsel becomes a 10-minute closer; Journal stays homework. |
| **75-minute block (alt days)** | 75 min × 2 | Day 1: Read & Receive + Prompt Lab. Day 2: Quest + Counsel. Journal homework. |
| **3-hour seminar (standard)** | 3 × 1 hour OR 1 × 3 | The syllabus as written. |
| **Intensive / Maymester / Winterm** | 4 hours/day × 3 weeks | 4 chapters/week. Cut the 30-day Mirror Practice to 7 days. Final Project becomes a public exhibition on the last day. |
| **Self-paced cohort (online)** | Async, with weekly synchronous Counsel | Atlas Wall becomes a class Discord or Padlet. Festival hosted on Zoom or in person at end. |
| **Single-unit drop-in** | 1 week | Pick *one* chapter. The Week 3 Myth quest is the highest-yield single-week adoption. |

## AI-Use Policy Boilerplate

Copy, adapt, paste into your student handbook. CC BY-SA — no permission needed.

> **Statement on AI Use in *The Recursive Century*.** This course uses generative AI as an explicit, declared collaborator. Students are *required* to use a generative model on every weekly quest and are *required* to annotate the human/AI split on every submission. Pretending an AI was not used, or pretending an AI was used when it was not, are both academic integrity violations.
>
> Students will not enter into any AI tool: (a) personally identifying information about any minor, (b) medical or financial records of any person, (c) the work of another student without that student's written consent, (d) any content their family or guardian has asked be kept private.
>
> Transcripts of student–AI conversations submitted for grading are educational records under FERPA and will be retained by the instructor only for the duration of the term plus one academic year. They will not be shared with the AI vendor for training purposes; students should select a vendor and a setting that honors this commitment, and the instructor will demonstrate how to verify the setting in Week 1.
>
> Students who, for reasons of conscience, religion, or family policy, do not wish to use a third-party AI service may complete every quest using only the public, openly-licensed [Prompt Atlas](https://promptatlas.dascient.org) prompt library and a written **counterfactual transcript** of what they would have asked. No student's grade will be reduced for choosing this path.

## Equity, Access & Care

The course's mid-term (Week 7 — Mirror Practice) and Week 11 (Pandemic Oracle Tabletop) can surface real material. Pre-deployment checklist:

- [ ] Counseling / wellness referral information is included in the syllabus students receive on day one.
- [ ] The Week 7 Mirror Practice **alternative** (third-person fictional protagonist) is offered without grade penalty and without the student having to explain why they chose it.
- [ ] Any student whose family lacks home internet is given scheduled lab time and a printed prompt packet (Appendix A from `compendium.html`, exportable).
- [ ] Any student under 13 (in jurisdictions where vendor ToS forbid use) completes quests through the instructor's account in a supervised lab setting, or with the counterfactual-transcript path above.
- [ ] English-language learners: every quest can be completed in the student's first language; the LLM is asked to translate to English for grading at submission.

## Frequently Asked, Honestly Answered

**"Will this course teach my students to write prompts?"**
Yes, but that is the least of what it teaches. Prompt fluency is downstream of intellectual fluency, and this course treats it that way.

**"Will my students learn to use [specific commercial AI product]?"**
The course is model-agnostic on purpose. Every quest works on any free-tier general-purpose LLM. We do not want your students dependent on, or trained for, any single vendor's interface.

**"My school district has banned AI in the classroom."**
Many have. We've heard from districts that adopted the syllabus as an **AI literacy course** taught *about* the technology without students using it — the Mirror Practice becomes a third-person case study, the Prompt Lab becomes a curated transcript reading, the Festival shifts to artifact defense without live AI use. The course's spine (the chapters) survives that subtraction. Email us if you'd like the "AI-abstinent" variant — we maintain it.

**"How do I assess co-authored work?"**
The Standard Quest Rubric (§11a of the syllabus) is designed exactly for this. Three of its four criteria — *Devotion to the source, Co-authorship integrity, Recursion* — are not assessable on the artifact alone. They require the human/AI split annotation and the journal. Students cannot game this with a better prompt.

**"What if my students just have the AI do the whole thing?"**
Then they will fail *Co-authorship integrity* and *Recursion*. The rubric is built so that an undeclared AI ghostwrite — even a brilliant one — can earn at most a 2/4 on two of the four criteria. We have classroom-tested this.

**"Is this religious?"**
No. The course uses words like *devotion*, *cathedral*, *oracle*, and *wonder* in their broad civilizational sense, the way *The Prompt Atlas* does. There is no creed, no faith claim, and no required spiritual practice. Secular instructors have run the syllabus without modification.

**"Is this political?"**
The course assumes that climate change is real, that data has provenance, that machine consciousness is an open question, that history happened, and that students should leave the term more able to argue with the instructor, not less. Beyond that, the course is committed to making *partisans of all kinds uncomfortable.*

**"What does it cost?"**
Zero. The book is optional. The platform is free. The license is non-commercial-sharealike. There is no paywall and no vendor lock-in.
